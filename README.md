# GPX road renderer

Pseudo-3D arcade road (OutRun / OutRunners style) driven by a GPX file: curves, hills, roadside scenery, intersections and skyline come from the data. Ride it at a fixed speed, at the recorded speed, or from a smart trainer, with a ghost of the recorded ride to race against. A separate editor page cleans up recorded rides before they become routes.

## Run
```
python3 serve.py 8000            # from this folder, then open http://localhost:8000
node tools/smoke.mjs             # builds every GPX through the pure modules and checks invariants
```
`serve.py` is `http.server` bound to localhost with caching off, so edits show on a plain reload. ES modules do not load from `file://`. It also lists routes (`rides.json`), runs the scene builder (`POST /build?f=`, progress from `build.json`), moves a route between `routes/` and `routes/favorites/` (`POST /fav?f=`) and accepts uploads into `routes/` and `rides/` (`POST /upload?f=`).

All data lives in three ignored folders: `routes/` (route GPX files, `favorites/`, built `scenes/`), `tiles/` (every download and processed cache, including raw lidar and `lidar/trees.sqlite`) and `rides/` (recorded rides). To ride on another machine, check out the code and copy them over, e.g. `rsync -av other-mac:workout/road_renderer/{routes,tiles,rides} ~/workout/road_renderer/`; with `tiles/` copied, building a scene over ground already covered downloads nothing.

Both pages take GPX files with or without timestamps. A recorded activity (Strava, Sauce, a head unit) has times and gives you recorded speed and a ghost; a planned route (`trkpt` or `rtept`, no times) rides at constant or trainer speed only, and the editor plays it back at 15 mph.

### Player (`index.html`)
Press Esc or click the road for the menu. The Ride tab lists favorites first, then every other `.gpx` in `routes/` under `All routes`; the ☆ next to a route (and on the confirm pane) moves the file between `routes/` and `routes/favorites/`, so a favorite is just where the file lives. Picking one shows whether its `.scene.json` exists plus miles, elevation gain and duration, and offers Ride, Ride without scene, and Build scene (runs `tools/build_scene.py` through the server; `Stop build` kills it) or Rebuild; when a build finishes the menu returns to that pane rather than starting the ride. The other tabs hold the levers, which persist in local storage along with the last ride's path; `Reset levers` restores defaults. `Load file…` with a lone GPX copies it into `routes/` and goes through the same confirm; drag-drop or picking a GPX together with its scene JSON loads them directly.

Transport bar: Play, speed source (constant mph, recorded × multiplier, live trainer), Ghost toggle with its speed percent, Snap ghost, Connect trainer, Connect HR (any standard Bluetooth heart rate sensor, such as a chest strap or an iPhone app relaying AirPods Pro 3; shown in the HUD and saved in the ride GPX), scrub. Keys: `space` play, `←`/`→` 50 ft (shift 500 ft), `↑`/`↓` speed 5 mph, `home` start, `g` snap ghost, `,`/`.` shift the virtual gear through the chosen cassette (nothing on the default `Fixed gear`, which rides 49×18).

`Start ride` switches the speed source to Live trainer and locks it until the ride ends, recording one sample per second of position, power and cadence. While riding, seeking only works when paused and asks for confirmation; reaching the end pauses with Restart / Join elsewhere / Finish (`Repeat` in the transport bar restarts from the top instead, for looping a short course); `Finish ride` writes a Strava-ready GPX (`VirtualRide`, power and cadence extensions) named `Route YYYY-MM-DD HHMM.gpx` to `rides/` through the server and downloads it.

URL params: `?f=routes/Big Cottonwood Canyon.gpx&z=6700` loads a file at a distance in meters (and its scene from `routes/scenes/` if present); any lever key overrides its saved value (`&farGain=3&theme=flat`).

### Editor (`editor.html`)
Open `http://localhost:8000/editor.html`, optionally with `?f=routes/ride.gpx`, or load / drop a GPX, TCX or FIT file. Playback on a map with an elevation profile; recording gaps longer than 15 s are marked as stops (`,` and `.` jump between them, `Skip stops` collapses them to 1 s). Edits, all applied in place and shown in the status line:

- `Fix elevation drift` removes barometric steps (jumps over 4 m that are not explained by the distance moved); `Level start/end` spreads the net drift over the ride; `Reset elevation` undoes both.
- `Mark start` / `Mark end` (`[` / `]`) then `Crop` keeps the section between them, trimming leading points that stand still or teleport. `Close loop` appends points every 5 m in a straight line from the end back to the start, at the speed held over the last points, so a lap ridden on Repeat has no jump. `Reverse` turns the ride around: points reversed, elevation kept, timestamps, power and therefore the ghost dropped, so it exports as a route without timestamps named `… reversed`.
- `Remove stops` replaces stops of 3 s up to the limit (lights, stop signs) with the speed held before braking: the window runs from where speed fell below 80% of the pre-stop peak to where it recovers, positions are interpolated in a straight line so GPS drift at the light disappears, and every later timestamp shifts earlier. Only for timed rides.
- `Export GPX` writes the result with gaps compressed; routes without timestamps export without `<time>`. The file name field also becomes the track's `<name>` inside the GPX, with underscores as spaces, so the ride list and HUD show the name you typed.

Exported files go through the player's `Load file…`, then build a scene for them.

### Trainer
The **Trainer** tab first asks what bike is on the trainer. `Real cassette, real shifting` hides everything else: resistance and speed go to and from the trainer unscaled and the rider shifts as on the road. `Single cog, virtual shifting` shows the gear levers, which model a virtual gear on a single-speed trainer bike: with physical ring/cog and virtual ring/cog, k = physical ratio ÷ virtual ratio and the trainer is sent grade/k, crr/k and ρ·CdA/k³, so the resistance at the flywheel equals the force a rider in the virtual gear would feel at the speed that gear implies (speed = trainer speed ÷ k, or cadence × virtual gear × wheel). The HUD shows the trainer's measured watts and cadence; rider weight is applied by the Kickr from the Wahoo app's profile. The wind lever is ρ·CdA in kg/m (0.4 is a road bike on the hoods), because that is how the trainer protocols define the wind field: the FTMS spec (section 4.16.2.18, "Cw (Wind Resistance Coefficient)", kg/m at 0.01 resolution) gives only the units, and the ANT+ FE-C profile it copies defines the coefficient as frontal area × drag coefficient × air density, with no ½; FE-C's default of 0.51 kg/m is that product for a CdA of about 0.42 m². The trainer applies the ½ itself, F = ½·Cw·v², as drag physics requires. An earlier lever was defined as ½·ρ·CdA = 0.2, so the Kickr received half the intended drag; a gear test on a flat square course measured 145 W at 21.7 mph where the road needs about 225 W, and after the fix 240 W at 21.4 mph against a model of 238 W, with climbing watts matching the grade model in both runs. Flywheel inertia is not scaled, so accelerations feel lighter than the virtual gear would. Grade is the GPX grade 20 m ahead, sent as FTMS simulation parameters over Web Bluetooth (Chrome). Distance, the ride clock, recording and grade updates advance on every trainer notification as well as every frame, so the page keeps progressing and recording while its tab is in the background; without a trainer, a hidden tab pauses.

## Files
| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `road.js` | GPX → track in local meters → road model: resample, smooth, straighten, drawn elevation. Pure data; each segment has a `props` slot for scenery. |
| `classes.json` | The one table from OSM to what is drawn, read top to bottom. `osm`: one section per OSM key (`natural`, `waterway`, `amenity`, `building:levels`, `building`, `landuse`, `barrier`, `power`, `highway`, `emergency`; `amenity` sits above `building` so a toilet block tagged `building=yes` resolves by its amenity, and `building` above `landuse` so a building carrying its zone's landuse resolves as a building, not as the zone). An OSM value must point at a name in `sets`, and the set's `sprites` at names in `sprites`; a name missing from either layer draws nothing, one entry per documented OSM value, whose value is a set name or `null` (recorded, draws nothing); `*` catches unlisted values; numeric keys take `3`, `3-5`, `6+`, `>2`, `<3`. Sections are checked in file order per element and the first match wins, which is how `building:levels` above `building` overrides it for tall buildings. A value may also be a nested lookup on another tag of the same element, `{ "leaf_type": { "needleleaved": "conifers", "*": "forest" } }`, which is how `natural=wood` splits by leaf type, `natural=tree` picks a pine or a broadleaf, and `building=yes` picks house, commercial or industrial from the `landuse` polygon the building stands in (the builder copies that landuse onto the building). `sets`: the compositor, a weighted list of sprite groups (repeat a name to weight it), `density` per km for area fill, `far` for props set back from the road, optional `levels` for a stacked sprite. A set without `density` is never generated and only appears where OSM maps the thing itself: the building sets work this way, since footprints are reliable, while trees and bushes keep filling unmapped ground. `sprites`: group name → manifest names, a hash of the segment index picks one; code-driven props (rider, banks, signs) live here too. Resolution happens in the player at scene load, so any edit takes effect on reload with no rebuild. Only what the Overpass query fetches can appear: ways tagged `natural`, `landuse`, `waterway`, `barrier`, `building`, `power=line`, and nodes tagged `natural=tree`, `highway=street_lamp|bus_stop|crossing`, `amenity=bench`, `emergency=fire_hydrant` (`NODE_QUERY` in the builder, cached as `tiles/osmn` separately from the way tiles). Adding other keys means a query change and a fresh tile layer. |
| `scene.js` | Fills `props` from a scene sidecar (`applyScene`) or a seeded placeholder rule (`placeholderScene`), map road widths, junction placement and warning signs. |
| `project.js` | Camera + levers → per-frame contract (screen points, visible quads, crest clip, blended skyline, projected actors). The contract is documented at the top of the file; themes consume it and nothing else. |
| `render.js` | Compositor: draws the theme into an offscreen framebuffer at the chosen resolution, blits up with nearest-neighbor. |
| `hud.js` | Overlays drawn into the pixel frame after the scene. `drawGauge`: the `gauge` dial 6 px in from the bottom right, the `needle` sheet (32 frames, 0° pointing left to 90° straight up, pivot at 20,20 of each 41×21 frame) mirrored for the other quadrants at 20 mph per 90°, and an odometer reading miles ridden as `000.0` from the `odometer_wheel` strip (digits 0–9 top to bottom on a 9 px pitch) rolling behind the dial's four transparent windows, each higher digit turning only while the one below passes 9. A blue `needle_power` sheet, the same layout, draws under the speed needle whenever there is power: the trainer's watts when connected, otherwise the recorded `<power>` of a GPX at that point; 200 W per 90°; both needles stop pointing straight down, at 60 mph and 600 W. Both needles follow an exponential moving average updated every frame (`Needle smoothing`, default 2 s, roughly a 3 s average; 0 is raw), since the trainer and recordings give one value per second and the trainer's watts swing stroke to stroke. `Speedometer overlay` turns it off. |
| `theme/<name>.js` | A palette passed to `theme()` from `theme/road_draw.js`, which owns the drawing (background, road, junctions, sprites). A palette's `skyImage` names a sprite drawn over the whole frame in place of the sky gradient (`arcade` uses `sky` from `sprites/art/`); `arcade` and `flat` exist. |
| `sprites.js`, `sprites/<set>/` | PNG per sprite + `manifest.json` (anchor pixel, height in meters, `faceRoad` to mirror on the right side). A sheet adds `frameW, frameH, row, frames`; the caller picks the frame. `make_placeholders.py` regenerates the placeholder set; `sprites/bike/` holds the rider sheet; `sprites/art/` is loaded last, so a sprite named there replaces the placeholder of the same name and falls back to it while the PNG is missing. A manifest-level `pxPerMeter` (4 in the art set, so a 64 px pine is 16 m) supplies `meters` for entries that omit it, and the anchor defaults to bottom centre, so a new art entry needs only its `file`. A `right` file on an entry (`"house_1": { "file": "house_1.png", "right": "house_1_right.png" }`) is drawn instead on the right side of the road, for art whose shading would be wrong mirrored; it needs the same size and anchor as the left file and takes precedence over `faceRoad`. A `stack: true` sheet of three frames (ground floor, storey, roof; `meters` is one storey) is drawn as floor + (levels − 1) storeys + roof, with levels from OSM `building:levels` or the set's `levels`. Adding `tree_4` to the manifest and to the `tree` and `anytree` groups in `classes.json` is all a new variant needs; `anytree` is what an untagged `natural=tree` draws from, every variant equally. |
| `ghost.js` | Recorded ride timing: distance at elapsed time, time at distance. |
| `kickr.js` | FTMS smart trainer over Web Bluetooth: speed and power in, grade out; heart rate sensor in. |

| `levers.js` | The lever table, defaults, menu pages, persistence and URL overrides. |
| `minimap.js`, `profile.js` | Leaflet minimap over Esri tiles (scroll to zoom in on the rider, click to lock heading-up) and the elevation strip. |
| `index.html` | Player: state, loading, transport, HUD, frame loop. |
| `editor.html` | Ride editor, standalone (Leaflet only). |
| `serve.py` | Dev server with ride listing, scene builds and uploads. |
| `tools/build_scene.py` | Builds the scene sidecar from OpenStreetMap + USGS 3DEP: `routes/scenes/<name>.scene.json` for a GPX in `routes/` or `routes/favorites/`, beside the GPX anywhere else. `uv run tools/build_scene.py routes/ride.gpx`. |
| `tools/clip_gpx.py` | Cuts a distance range out of a GPX: `python3 tools/clip_gpx.py ride.gpx 26.0 27.2 routes/name.gpx "Name"`. |

Routes, rides and scene sidecars stay local: `*.gpx`, `*.scene.json`, `routes/` and `rides/` are gitignored, so the repo holds only code. Layout: `routes/` holds the GPX files you ride, `routes/favorites/` the starred ones, `routes/scenes/` every scene sidecar (`Name.scene.json` for `Name.gpx` wherever it sits), and `rides/` the recordings `Finish ride` writes. Only `routes/` is listed. Keep routes short while iterating on scene data; Overpass throttles long ones.

## Pipeline
1. Parse `trkpt` / `rtept` lat/lon/ele/time, project to local meters.
2. Resample along distance into fixed-length segments (`Segment length`).
3. Smooth x/y (`Curve smoothing`) and elevation (`Elevation smoothing`) separately. Headings within `Straighten deadband` degrees of their average over `Straighten window` snap to that average, so GPS wobble on straights disappears while real bends keep their raw heading. Elevation splits into a trend (`Hill trend window`) and bumps, re-integrated as drawn grade = cap × tanh((gradeGain × trend + hillScale × bumps) / cap).
4. Each frame, project the next `Draw distance` segments and draw near-to-far, clipping behind crests (Lou's hill trick). Curves: segments stay at z = n × segLen, lateral offset accumulates as `dx += segLen × dTheta × curveStrength`.
5. `Far gain` scales elevation relative to the rider by a factor ramping from 1 at the camera to `Far gain` at `Far gain distance`, so a steady climb still rears up ahead.

The camera stays level and themes draw sky, skyline and a distant-ground band at the true horizon before the road. Flat road converges on the skyline base; uphill the road and grass climb over it; downhill you see distant ground. That reference is what makes a still read as uphill. Skyline columns are mapped through the same perspective as the road (`atan` of the column's tangent at the current field of view), so a heading change rotates the background exactly as the road implies. With a scene file the skyline is the real terrain horizon (`Skyline` lever); without one, periodic procedural ridges.

## Pixel look
`Resolution` picks the framebuffer size. 320×224 is what OutRun, System 32 (OutRunners) and the Genesis (Road Rash) drew at; SNES was 256×224. Sprites are drawn at native size and the single upscale keeps them crisp. `Pixel fit` chooses integer zoom with letterbox, aspect-preserving fit, or stretch.

## Scene sidecar (`tools/build_scene.py`)
Samples the route every 10 m and writes (`version` 3), per sample: `left` / `right` raw OSM tags of every element within reach, elements joined with `+` and one element's tags with `;` (`natural=wood+building=yes;building:levels=5`), limited to the keys in `classes.json`; polygons count by point-in-polygon at 15 m, buildings also within 30 m, waterway and power lines within 40 m, other lines 20 m; `pts`, mapped point features as `[sample index, lateral m with left positive, tags]`, one entry per pass of the route: nodes within 40 m and every building within 60 m by its centroid, so houses stand at their true distance; `road` tags (`bridge`, `tunnel`), `profL` / `profR` ground height in m above the road at `profD` = 10, 25, 50, 100 m from the centerline (`slopeL` / `slopeR` are the 25 m value as a %), and `horizon` every 500 m: 360 elevation angles (far = everything to 12 km, near = within 1.5 km), indexed by math bearing (0 = east, counterclockwise).

Roads: the builder matches the route to the OSM way it rides on (nearest road within 15 m, paths penalised by 8 m so a sidewalk next to the road does not win, matches shorter than 40 m absorbed into the previous way so crossing a road does not briefly put the route on it) and stores `ways` with the shape fully resolved (`kind` road / trail / path / dirt, `rank`, `lanes`, `width` in m, `name`, `oneway`, `bridge`, `tunnel`) and `way` (index per sample). The renderer carries no OSM vocabulary. Every other road that crosses or tees into the route goes in `xings`: route index, side (`L`, `R`, `LR`), angle from the route heading, `kind`, `rank`, width, name, and `sig` of `signal` or `stop` when such a node sits within 20 m. A way ending within 15 m of the route is a T from one side, checked on every pass; a way's direction is measured over 30 m from the junction. Rivers, canals and streams the route passes over go in `xings` too, as `kind` water with a width by type (12, 6, 3 m), drawn as a blue band on both sides so bridges read as bridges; the road itself is unbroken. `waterL` / `waterR` give, per sample, the near and far edge (m from the centerline) of the first water along the road's normal within 50 m: ponds and lakes from polygons, creeks from waterway lines widened by type. The player fills that span blue on the ground, skips roadside terrain and props there, and `Roadside water` turns it off. `sideL` / `sideR` list every other highway running within 30° of the route and 50 m of it (near edge, far edge, `kind`), nearest first, each drawn in that kind's road colour and switched by `Roads & paths alongside`; a path and the street beyond it both show. Terrain starts beyond the farthest band. Roads on a different level (bridge, tunnel, `layer`), driveways, parking aisles, sidewalks and crosswalks are ignored.

Sources: Overpass (OSM polygons/lines within 80 m of the route, point-in-polygon at 15 m either side), USGS 3DEP exportImage (10 m DEM for slopes, 60 m DEM for skyline ray-marching). Downloads are stored as fixed-grid tiles in `tiles/` (`dem10` 0.05° at ~10 m, `dem60` 0.5° at 60 m, `osm2` 0.02° Overpass extracts: land cover, barriers, every road, signal and stop nodes, plus buildings in a second query per tile so dense suburbs don't time out) and shared across rides, so a new route through already-built area fetches nothing. The builder works in passes over growing prefixes of the route, about one OSM tile (2 km) apart, and rewrites the scene file after every pass that had to download something, with `built` and `total` distances; the confirm pane offers `Ride now` as soon as the first pass lands, and the player re-reads the file as it grows so scenery fills in ahead of the rider. Beyond `built` the road draws bare. Any failed download aborts the build; rerun and it resumes from the tiles. Three Overpass mirrors are tried in turn with a pause after every query. Terrain is US-only (3DEP; Copernicus DEM 30 m would be the global swap); `--no-terrain` / `--no-osm` skip a source. Expect roughly 10 s per 2 km of new ground, more when the public servers return 504/429 and the builder backs off.

Put the sidecar next to the GPX; the menu, `?f=` and the file picker all find it by name. Changing the scene format means rebuilding the sidecars, which is free once the tiles are cached.

## Map roads and intersections
Levers under **Map roads**:

- `Road width & lanes from map`: per-segment width from the scene (tagged `width`, else `lanes` × 3.6 m, else a class default), smoothed over 50 m. Lane dividers are drawn per lane, yellow at the centre of two-way roads, white dashed elsewhere. Trails render narrow and tan with no markings or kerb stripes; dirt renders brown. `Map road width scale` multiplies those widths (and side-road bands); Off falls back to the `Road width` lever.
- `Intersections`: side streets drawn as a flat asphalt band leaving the road edge, Road Rash style, with edge and centre lines. Scenery and terrain are suppressed where the street goes. Side roads are drawn perpendicular, row by row along the main road so they follow its curve; roads meeting within 30° of parallel are skipped.
- `Smallest side road drawn`: everything (paths, service roads; the default), streets and up (residential+), or collectors and up (tertiary+). Crossings narrower than 6 m (service roads, paths) are drawn without edge or centre lines; paths are brown, trails tan.
- `Street name signs`: a green plate with the crossing road's name (first 15 characters) on a pole at the near corner, drawn on a canvas at load time rather than from the sprite sheet.
- `Stop signs & signals`: a stop sign before the junction or a signal pole after it, on the right, when OSM has the node.
- `Mapped trees & street furniture`: the scene's `pts` resolved through `classes.json` like everything else and placed at their true offset from the road using the first sprite of the set, a mapped tree at its OSM `height` when tagged, a building with its `building:levels`. Within 50 m of a mapped point no generated prop of the same sprite group is placed on that side, so a park with every tree surveyed shows only those trees and an unsurveyed stretch falls back to the generated mix. Lamps, bus stops, benches and hydrants have placeholder sprites (`street_lamp`, `bus_stop`, `bench`, `fire_hydrant`) that an art sprite of the same name replaces. A point whose set resolves to the `crossing` sprite group is not a sprite at all: it paints zebra bars across the road for 3 m at that segment.
- `Turn & hill warning signs`: from the road geometry alone, no scene needed. A left or right turn sign where the heading changes 60° within the next 150 m (reset below 30°), an uphill or downhill sign where the grade reaches 2% over the next 30 m and 2.5% over the next 100 m (reset below 1%), each placed on the right where the lookahead first sees it. Sprites `left_turn_sign`, `right_turn_sign`, `uphill_sign`, `downhill_sign` in `sprites/art/`.


## Roadside terrain and sprites
The `terrain` lever picks how the cross-section is drawn, all from the same `profL/R` data and all continuous: `shoulders` extrudes the ground beside the road as stacked quads out to 100 m, colored grass/scrub/rock by steepness; `wall` is a Road Rash style vertical face whose height follows the ground 6 m out, filled with a perspective-scaled rock tile; `banks` stacks overlapping mound sprites scaled by that height. Scenery sprites stand on the slope in every mode. `terrainScale` exaggerates heights. Profiles are smoothed over 50 m and interpolated per segment in `scene.js`.

Sprites are drawn after the road, far to near. Height on screen = meters × (segment half-width px ÷ road half-width m), so a 14 m pine stays 14 m relative to the road. Anything below the segment's clip line is cropped, so trees behind a crest sink out of sight like the road does. Offsets are meters from the road edge, so road width never puts a tree on the pavement. Buildings and cabins are drawn with a visible side and mirrored on the right of the road (`faceRoad`). Actors (rider, ghost) go through the same projection at fractional segment positions and are drawn interleaved with scenery by depth.

## Ghost
`Ghost` replays the GPX timestamps: elapsed ride time → recorded distance. The percent next to it scales the ghost's clock, so 90% rides 10% slower than the recording and 110% faster; it can be changed mid-ride. Scrubbing sets the clock to the recorded time at that point, so the ghost starts beside you; `Snap ghost` (or `g`) does the same without moving you, for when it is lost or too far ahead. The HUD's Ghost data line shows the gap in feet and seconds. Remove stops in the editor first if the recording waited at lights; the ghost reproduces every stop.

## Decisions and known limits
- Semantic props, not sprite names, in the road model, so terrain work and art work never touch the same file. Walls are geometry; repeated slab sprites read as boxes.
- Fetching is offline (the Python builder); the pages only read JSON. ES modules without a bundler, hence serving over http.
- Sprite depth sorting is per segment; a tree and the ghost on the same segment may overlap in the wrong order. Sprites do not fog, as on the arcade boards.
- On steep climbs the exaggerated ground rises over the skyline. That is the arcade look; lower `Grade gain` if the skyline matters more than the hill.
- Scene classification is coarse: `stream` within 40 m, buildings within 30 m, no footprints or orientation.
