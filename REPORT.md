# Build report

## What exists now
- **Road model** (`road.js`): GPX → fixed-length segments with smoothed curves, trend/bump split elevation, tanh grade cap. Unchanged in behaviour from before the split, now pure data with a `props` slot per segment and a `skyline` slot.
- **Projection** (`project.js`): one frame contract for everything downstream. Adds crest `clip` per segment, blended terrain `skyline` for the current position, and projected `actors`.
- **Compositor** (`render.js`): offscreen framebuffer at 320×224 (or 256×224, 384×224, 480×270, native), integer-zoom letterbox by default, nearest-neighbor upscale. The pixel look is this one rule, not the art.
- **Themes** (`theme/`): palette + sprite manifest + kind→sprite table. Shared drawer does sky gradient, skyline, distant ground, road, walls, sprites, actors. Two palettes: arcade, flat.
- **Sprites** (`sprites/`): one PNG per sprite with anchor and height in meters; placeholder set generated by `make_placeholders.py` (pine, tree, bush, rock, sign, building, cabin, cliff, rider, ghost). Scale on screen derives from the road's own projection, so meters are the only unit an artist sets.
- **Scenery** (`scene.js`): fills `props` from a scene sidecar or a seeded placeholder rule. Owns the land-class → kind rules and densities. Emits continuous `wall` props where OSM says cliff or the ground rises ≥ 80% beside the road; the drawer extrudes those as OutRun-style canyon walls.
- **Scene builder** (`tools/build_scene.py`, `uv run`): Overpass for land cover, water, buildings, barriers, bridges, tunnels at 15 m either side; USGS 3DEP 10 m for cross slopes; USGS 3DEP 60 m ray-marched for a real 360° skyline every 500 m (near ≤ 1.5 km and far ≤ 12 km layers). Cached per request so reruns are free. Pure numpy + urllib, no GIS stack.
- **Skyline mapping**: columns go through `atan` of the screen tangent at the current FOV, so a heading change rotates the background exactly as the road implies; verified to about a pixel across 56° of heading.
- **Ghost** (`ghost.js`): recorded time ↔ distance from GPX timestamps, first-arrival through pauses. Drawn as a translucent rider on the road, dot on the minimap, line on the profile, gap in meters and m:ss in the HUD. Scrubbing sets the clock to the recorded time, so the ghost starts beside you.
- **Trainer** (`kickr.js`): FTMS over Web Bluetooth, speed in, GPX grade 20 m ahead out as simulation grade. Ported from the replay page one folder up; cadence/fixed-gear speed was left out.
- **Player** (`index.html`): levers (persisted), URL params (`f`, `z`, any lever), multi-file load (GPX + scene JSON), constant / recorded / live speed, keyboard, HUD.
- **Clips** (`clips/`): S-curve (1.2 km), canyon mouth, valley flat, each with sidecar. `tools/clip_gpx.py` cuts more.

## Decisions
- **Level camera + fixed horizon reference** is the uphill cue. Pitch follow default 0; grade gain 3, far gain 1.5 at 50 m. Flat converges on the skyline base, climbs cover it.
- **Semantic props, not sprite names, in the road model.** Terrain work and art work never touch the same file.
- **Walls are geometry, sprites are not.** Repeated slab sprites read as boxes; an extruded polygon reads as a canyon wall.
- **Fetching is offline** (Python sidecar), the page only reads JSON. Overpass is rate-limited; a 130 km ride is minutes and fails intermittently, short clips take seconds.
- **ES modules, no bundler**, which means serving over http. Worth it for dynamic theme import and fetchable manifests.
- **Placeholder art is procedural** so the pipeline could be proven before any hunting for assets.

## Known limits
- Overpass public server throttles; the tool retries and skips a failed chunk rather than aborting. A private Overpass or a bulk OSM extract would remove this.
- Terrain sources are US-only (3DEP). Copernicus DEM 30 m is the global swap.
- On steep climbs the exaggerated ground rises over the skyline. That is the arcade look, and it hides the real walls at the same time; lower `Grade gain` if the skyline matters more than the hill.
- Sprite depth sorting is per segment and actors interleave by segment; a tree and the ghost on the same segment may overlap in the wrong order.
- Sprites do not fog. Arcade machines did not either.
- Scene classification is coarse: `stream` within 40 m, buildings within 30 m, no building footprints or orientation, no road width/lanes from OSM yet (the data is fetched, `highway` tags are not used).
- Grade sim to the trainer is fire-and-forget; no gear/cadence speed option.

## Suggestions
Art
- Replace the placeholder set with a 16-bit pack (Kenney CC0 or OpenGameArt) by writing a new manifest folder and pointing a theme at it. Two or three sizes per sprite would stop far-off shimmer.
- Turn the skyline into a real pixel panorama: the DEM profile can mask a drawn mountain strip so the silhouette stays accurate.
- Road texture: OutRun's road is just the alternating bands you have; a center-line pattern and shoulder gravel are palette entries away.
- Ground bands: alternate colors per land class (water left = blue band) using `props`, no new data needed.

Scene data
- Use OSM `highway` tags already fetched: `lanes`, `width`, `oneway`, `surface`, `maxspeed` for signs. Bridges and tunnels are tagged in `road` props but not drawn; a tunnel darkening and bridge rails are cheap theme features.
- ESA WorldCover 10 m for vegetation density outside OSM coverage.
- Side slope downward (`slope ≤ -60`) could draw a drop-off (no ground band on that side, valley color instead).
- Snow line from elevation, season from the GPX date.

Feel
- Camera lateral lean on curves (shift the vanishing point with curvature) is one line in `project.js` and adds a lot of motion feel.
- Speed-dependent FOV and stripe scroll.
- Per-segment fog color from time of day.

Ghost and ride
- Multiple ghosts (several activities on the same clip), colored.
- Record the live ride to TCX as the replay page does; the sample loop is already there to copy.
- A finish line sprite and split times at clip end.

Tooling
- A tiny "clip picker": click two points on the profile, export a clip GPX and build its sidecar from the page by calling the Python tool. Keeps iteration inside the app.
